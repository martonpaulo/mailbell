Do not rely on it for messages you cannot afford to miss. It supplements Gmail; it does not replace checking Gmail.

2. Public beta and Google's review status

Mailbell is currently in public beta, and its Google OAuth client has not been verified by Google. As a result:

  • Google will display an “unverified app” warning screen during sign-in. You must choose to continue past it yourself.
  • Google limits unverified apps to 100 new users. Once that cap is reached, new users cannot connect an account until verification completes. No unlimited or guaranteed availability is promised while the app is unverified.
  • Google may change, restrict, suspend, or revoke the app's API access at any time, at its sole discretion and without notice to you or to me. That would stop Mailbell from working, and it is outside my control.

3. Google Workspace and managed accounts

If your Gmail account belongs to a Google Workspace organization, your administrator can block Mailbell from connecting, either specifically or through a policy that blocks unverified third-party apps. Sign-in will simply fail, and there is nothing the app can do about it. Requesting an exception is between you and your administrator.

You are responsible for making sure your use of Mailbell complies with any policies that apply to your account.

4. Your Google account and access

You are responsible for granting and revoking Mailbell's access to your Google account. You grant it through Google's own consent screen.

Those are two separate actions, and Mailbell only performs one of them:

  • Removing the account in Mailbell's Settings stops the app: it deletes the stored tokens from your Keychain along with the account's local state, and stops monitoring. It does not tell Google anything.
  • Revoking access at your Google Account's third-party access page ends the authorization itself, at the source. Do this if you want the grant gone, not just the app stopped.

The Privacy Policy describes both steps in order.

You are responsible for your own Google account security and for the consequences of connecting an account you do not control.

5. No backend, no service

Mailbell is software you run, not a service I operate. There is no Mailbell server processing your Gmail. No component of this project receives, stores, or processes your mail. Consequently there is no uptime commitment, no support-level agreement, and no account with me to suspend or terminate.

What the app does access is described in the Privacy Policy, which forms part of these terms.

6. Updates

Mailbell can update itself using Sparkle, and automatic checks are on by default. Updates are signed and verified before they are installed. You can disable automatic checks in Settings → Updates. Older versions may stop working if Google or Apple changes something, and no support is provided for versions other than the latest release.
